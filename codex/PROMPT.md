You are transcribing two pages of a 16th-century French cipher letter, symbol by symbol, from images.

Attached images:
- 00_reference_sheet.png: a reference sheet of symbol shapes. Each row starts with a symbol NAME (red) followed by real examples cut from letters in the same hand and cipher. Use these names.
- The cipher lines, in order. File names give the line id: r_L01a = recto line 1, left half; r_L01b = recto line 1, right half; … r_L31a/b; then v_L01a/b … v_L08a/b = verso lines 1–8. The left and right halves of a line overlap a little in the middle: do not count a symbol twice. Each image may show partial strokes of the neighbouring lines at its top edge: read only the main line (the complete line across the image).

Task: for each line, write the sequence of cipher symbols exactly as they appear, left to right, using the symbol names. Do NOT try to decipher or guess the meaning, and do not use any knowledge of what the text might say: transcribe shapes only.

Rules:
- Use the names on the reference sheet, plus the extra names listed below.

- The slanting word divider stroke: /
- Small letters written above the line (interlinear additions): ^(names), e.g. ^(8 delta).
- A symbol struck out with a line: {name}.
- Isolated small dots between symbols are not symbols; ignore them (except where a dot is part of a named shape, e.g. dots, :, Id, xd).
- If unsure of a symbol, add ? after the name (e.g. r?). An unreadable symbol: ?
- A symbol that matches no name: <short description>.
- Look-alike pairs to watch: r and x; 8 and 8s; S, ls and hs; f, f2 and a long f without bar; th and 4t; #, 4t and tt; w (three humps) and w2 (two humps); 2o (one symbol, two loops) versus "a 0"; the number .24. versus "2 q+"; z9 and 9; phi (two loops on a stem) versus pdot (one small loop on a stem); curl (large backslash with a small o) versus curlo (a stroke whose foot curls to the right).
